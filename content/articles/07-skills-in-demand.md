The issues raised in this document have been discussed with several colleges and IT training vendors, all of whom have expressed interest in general IT training

#### Global IT Skills Shortage
The shortage of IT skills is a global issue, impacting the UK in all sectors as well.

#### Purpose of IT
Two key purposes of IT are to enhance productivity, to streamline business processes – often via Digital Transformation (DTX) - and reduce human error.

#### Current IT Skill Levels
The UK ranks 45th in Coursera's 2024 IT skill levels list, just below Honduras; the US is 69th, just below Panama. At the same time, the UK is 25<sup>th</sup> in Europe for IT skills.

#### High Failure Rate of IT Projects
Approximately 70% of IT projects fail, ranging from minor issues to total disasters. These include digital transformation and modernisation, current ‘flavours of the month’.

#### Inadequate IT Education
Current IT education is largely focused on computer science (CS) and others on PC-based systems (Windows/Linux/MacOS), neglecting mainframes, High Performance Computing (HPC), and large systems’ operating systems and methodologies. The latter are crucial to success.

This educational gap contributes to the decades-old high failure rate of IT projects.

#### Importance of Mainframes
This is covered elsewhere on this website.

#### HPC's Expanding Role
HPC has evolved beyond scientific computing into areas like AI, financial modelling, life sciences, and other compute intensive research fields.

### Consequences of Low IT Skills
#### Economic Impact
Inefficient IT usage leads to significant financial waste and hinders productivity gains.

The lack of skills in mainframes and HPC results in unrealised potential in critical industries.

#### Dependence on IT
Large companies are heavily reliant on IT; any disruption in service or quality can have severe repercussions on their operations and stakeholders.

### Proposed Solution
#### Reforming IT Education
Current university and school IT education is thorough but misaligned with modern workplace needs, particularly regarding the mainframe and enterprise systems in general.

Much of the teaching is very theoretical and often cannot answer the questions ‘so what?’

Academic teaching often fails to address the practical skills required in today's IT environments, such as methodologies, resilience and best practices.

#### Workplace-Based Learning
The pragmatic approach is to educate individuals within the workplace through apprenticeships, where skills are directly relevant.

Skills based IT employment is gradually replacing certificates and degrees as indicators of suitability.

#### Pre-Apprenticeship Training
There is a critical need for comprehensive general IT courses as a precursor to apprenticeships, job entries, or specialisations.

These courses should provide an overview of the IT ecosystem, enabling students to make informed career choices. Current education denies students of knowledge of a significant part of the IT terrain.

The apprentice with the broadest shoulders is able to carry a heavier load than the skinny one, however tall.

### Dispelling Myths
#### Broader IT Awareness
IT is not just about coding, algorithms, computational thinking (whatever that means) or PC- and Windows-centric knowledge. A well-rounded IT education exposes students to a wide range of career opportunities, beyond just programming, desktop environments.

By addressing these educational gaps and misconceptions, we can better prepare the workforce for the diverse and evolving demands of the IT industry and its users.

The March of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

AI is making inroads into many areas of business and into IT itself and as a result, the jobs spectrum is changing. The use of task automation and AI in IT means that much routine data analysis can be done by the use of these features.

This trend will accentuate the need for a solid basic understanding of IT so that the inevitable job *mutation* can be coped with by IT employees. People may run from this mindset change but cannot hide from it.

This makes the case for a comprehensive IT grounding, which complements, and sometimes replaces current education which does no cover the IT knowledge need in the modern workplace.

This is the *raison d’etre* for the Introduction to Modern IT course.

### Summary
Before you teach a child to swim the crawl, backstroke etc., It is imperative to make sure they feel comfortable in the water environment by sitting in it, splashing around and even putting their heads under the water.

A similar principle applies in IT, where a familiarity with the IT ‘water’ is an excellent way to feel you way to higher levels of knowledge. Such general knowledge is like the tiled floor of a swimming pool; the child feels safe as long as they can stand on it when they stop swimming.
