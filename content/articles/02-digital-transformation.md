Digital transformation (DTX) is about as well understood by IT guru and IT ignorant alike as the equations in Einstein's General Relativity theory. This is no surprise because DTX means different things to almost everybody. The RESOURCE, part of the '*Introduction to Modern IT*' might clear up some of the mystery.

In essence, DTX is a full-on, all-embracing commitment by an organisation to use computers as far as feasible for automating business processes, via education, a clear path to this end related to all concerned and a skills, facilities infrastructure and widely understood modus operandi to achieve these goals to support this ’digital transformation’ plan.

It is not a ‘*let’s write a programme and bang it on the compute*r’, nor is it a one-off exercise but an organisation’s way of life, defined by *cradle to grave* processes, from business need to IT implementation and operation.

### What is It?
> *Definition 1***: ‘**Digital t**r**ansformation is the process of adoption and implementation of [digital technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) by an organisation to add new or modify existing products, services and operations by the means of translating business processes involved in these areas into a computerised format.’

The [Chartered Institute of Procurement & Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_Institute_of_Procurement_%26_Supply) (CIPS) defines digitalisation as;

> *Definition 2***:** ‘The practice of redefining models, functions, operations, processes and activities by leveraging technological advancements to build an efficient digital business environment – one where gains (operational and financial) are maximised, and costs and risks are minimised’.

### What Exactly is Digital Transformation?
Digital transformation (DTX) is the integration of digital technology into all areas of a business, fundamentally changing how that business operates. Its aim is to deliver value to the customers and organisation benefits. It should also have a positive effect on internal productivity, which is one reason for using computers.

It also involves a cultural change that requires organisations to continually challenge the way they operate, experiment, and learn to cope with, and take lessons from failure.

Digital transformation is interpreted in more ways than any other concept in IT. They vary from, ‘*let’s bang everything we have on the computer’*, to ‘let’s innovate our business’, both old and new processes and implement them on the computer, where benefit to the business accrues.

Digital transformation provides a valuable opportunity for core business functions, such as finance and HR, to move away from manual processes and work to automate key areas like payroll. It is also an opportunity to review and update old processes and create new ones, both in the light of changing business environments or organisation-relevant legislation.

A 2023 McKinsey report states; ‘In digital and AI transformations, start with the problem, not the technology. For digital and AI transformations to succeed, companies need to understand the problems they want to solve, and rewire their organisations for continuous innovation.’

The final part of this resources shows the value of getting it right; the cost of getting it wrong does not bear thinking about.

### What is It Not?
> ‘Although sometimes used synonymously, the term digital transformation is different from digitisation and digitalisation.
>
> *Digitisation* is simply the process of converting analogue information ( such as information stored in physical documents) into digital information. Conversely, *digitalisation* is more business process focused, using digital information to improve work efficiency. Finally, *digital transformation* takes digitalisation to its logical conclusion, applying digital technologies to every aspect of business and extending the associated benefits across the whole organisation.’

- [servicenow.com](https://www.servicenow.com/uk/products/strategic-portfolio-management/what-is-digital-transformation.html)

What DTX is **not** is putting what non-IT work a company already has onto the computer, for better or for worse. Neither is it moving the workload from a legacy system, a vintage mainframe, to a brand new, all-singing and dancing computer, the internet-oriented Hokey-Cokey 2000.

*Analogy:* You cannot make a silk purse (new, business-oriented processes) from a pig’s ear (old outdated processes) by switching technology platforms. This is like moving a sick patient from a scruffy room with an old lumpy bed to a new airy room with a modern bed with numerous electronic features. The patient is still sick.

### The Four Pillars of DTX
Many sources cite four pillars of digital transformation: customer experience transformation, business processes transformation, business model transformation, and organisational transformation. These pillars represent the key areas an organisation must address to successfully become digital-first by creating new revenue streams, optimising operations, enhancing customer interactions, and making structural changes.

  - *Customer Experience Transformation*:  This involves personalising customer interactions through data-driven solutions and creating seamless, omnichannel experiences to improve satisfaction and loyalty.

  - *Business Processes Transformation*:  This pillar focuses on digitalising and optimising existing business processes, from operations to decision-making, to increase efficiency and improve performance.

  - *Business Model Transformation*:  This pillar is about evolving the business model to generate new revenue streams through innovative products and services, and global expansion.

  - *Organisational Transformation*:  This pillar addresses the necessary structural changes at both the strategic and operational levels, including shifting to a more agile, digital-first mindset and building the right talent and culture.

### Detailed Activities
#### 1. Define Strategy and End Point
  - *Define objectives*: Clarify what digital transformation means for your organisation—e.g., improving customer experience, operational efficiency, innovation and organisation-specific aims.

  - *Identify Sponsor(s):* Management with a vested interest in DTX for their business. DTX formula: *No Sponsor = Failure*

  - *Assess current state*: Evaluate existing systems, processes, and digital maturity.

  - *Engage stakeholders*: Involve leadership, IT, and business units to align on goals [Karbon](https://karbonhq.com/resources/7-steps-for-planning-and-executing-a-digital-transformation/) [Digital Adoption](https://www.digital-adoption.com/digital-transformation-roadmap/).

#### 2. DTX Readiness Assessment
  - Identify gaps between current capabilities and desired outcomes.

  - Assess organisational culture, skills, and infrastructure.

  - Determine legacy and other systems constraints and integration, and CSF needs.

> See: <https://changemanagementinsight.com/the-6-stages-of-digital-transformation-a-step-by-step-guide/>

#### 3. Roadmap Development
  - Create a *digital transformation roadmap* with clear milestones, review points timelines, and KPIs.

  - Appoint project manager(s), method, milestones, roles and responsibilities, reviews etc.

  - Prioritise DTX initiatives based on organisation impact and feasibility.

  - Set up change management and communication plans. [Digital Adoption](https://www.digital-adoption.com/digital-transformation-roadmap/).

#### 4. Technology Selection and Design
  - Choose appropriate technologies (cloud, AI, automation, etc.) aligned with goals.

  - Design scalable architecture and integration strategies.

  - Ensure cybersecurity and compliance considerations are embedded.

This is an important step which is carried out to see whether the current IT status and business processes are fit to handle the planned transformations in areas:

  - > *IT systems’* capacity, currency (if old, modernisation) and longevity

  - > *Skills upgrades* needed to achieve the business goals

  - > *IT rationalisation and consolidation* efforts to provide a manageable infrastructure, fit to support digital transformation

  - > Assess the *best development method* to support the development, delivery and maintenance of applications (CI/CD, DevOps, waterfall etc.); see above for details

  - > A solid *change management* method rigorously applied to all changes, business and IT

  - > Review and *upgrade methods and tools* for cybersecurity (including interactions with third parties), high availability, disaster recovery, process automation (business and IT), IT management and other items deemed important for business success

  - > Plan for a DTX **pilot** to verify the transformation method’s effectiveness

  - > From all the above, and anything else that arises, develop the organisation’s DTX business and IT plans or updates.

On this note, Garter, in their Gartner Priorities Navigator, recommend this with the words; ‘*Build and optimize infrastructure and cloud platform*s’ in implementing a strategic operating model. [This is exactly what DTX is.]

#### 5. Implementation and Deployment
  - Begin with pilot projects or phased rollouts.

  - Train teams and ensure user adoption.

  - Monitor progress and adjust based on feedback and performance metrics

#### 6. Change Management and Cultural Shift
  - Foster a digital-first mindset across the organisation.

  - Address resistance through training, incentives, and leadership support.

  - Promote collaboration and agility

#### 7. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Optimisation
  - Track KPIs and ROI of digital initiatives.

  - Continuously refine processes and technologies.

  - Scale successful pilots across departments

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
  - *Skipping stakeholder alignment* or underestimating cultural resistance.

  - *Overcomplicating tech choices* without clear business value.

  - *Neglecting change management*, which is often the biggest barrier to success. Changes don’t come much bigger than DTX

### What Does DTX Mean to the Business?
Digital transformation is the process of fundamentally changing how a business operates by examination of current business processes, designing new ones to cater for change in the areas of:

  - > Changes in the business scenarios that the organisation inhabits, e,g. finance, health, chemical, media etc.

  - > Compliance with edicts and best practices of that business scenario

  - > Competing with rivals

  - > Increasing market share, customer satisfaction and employee loyalty and productivity

This is generally achieved by one, some or all of the following:

  - > Stop thinking of ‘carrying on the business as usual’ but instil an ‘agility mentality’ in the workforce which seeks constant evaluation of processes and change where necessary

  - > Make changes to improve in the areas of better products and services, together with more effective and efficient working practices

  - > Integrate these changes into technology as appropriate. Do not fit the business change to the trendiest technology but do the reverse.

  - > It is not a simple exercise as the quoted failure rates indicate and it follows the rule: *‘Fools rush in where angels fear to tread’*.

In essence, DTX is a mind-changing exercise, not in the ways practiced by dictatorships, but one to facilitate, and cater for business and IT change.

### What Benefits Accrue from Quality DTX?
  - Increased efficiency and productivity:  Automation of manual tasks, optimised workflows, and the use of digital tools reduce errors and enable employees to focus on higher-value activities.

  - Improved customer experience:  Personalised interactions, faster service delivery, and more convenient channels lead to greater customer satisfaction and loyalty.

  - Enhanced decision-making:  Access to real-time data and analytics allows for more informed and strategic decisions across the organisation.

  - Greater agility and responsiveness:  Businesses can adapt more quickly to market changes and customer needs.

  - Drives innovation and new opportunities, hence new products and revenue

  - Cost reduction by automating processes and improving efficiency, companies can lower operational costs.

  - Improved collaboration:  Digital tools enhance communication and teamwork, even among remote employees.

  - Stronger security:  Modern digital infrastructure can lead to improved data security across the organisation.

  - Better sustainability:  Digitising processes can reduce waste and promote more environmentally friendly practices.

### Key Aspects of Digital Transformation
*Starting Point:* You wouldn’t start along car journey with a car which is not fit for purpose; you will need to get it into shape for the long journey; ditto DTX.

*Integration of Technology***:** This involves adopting a wide range of digital tools and technologies, such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, automation, data analytics, and mobile technologies.

*Business Transformation***:** Digital transformation goes beyond just the technology. It's about using these tools to rethink and redesign business processes, preach the new culture, and create workflows for greater efficiency, agility, and innovation.

*Customer Focus***:** A core driver of digital transformation is understanding and meeting the ever-evolving needs of customers in a digital age. This often involves creating more personalised and interactive customer experiences.

*Continuous Improvement***:** Digital transformation is an ongoing process. Businesses need to be adaptable and embrace a culture of continuous learning and improvement to keep pace with the changing technological landscape.

*Development Methods:* The ‘free for all’ development of applications and related activities is not the recommended way to implement DTX as this would resemble a Western saloon brawl and achieve little. You need to make a choice based on *best fit* to your DTX project, not any old method.

Making the assumption that the current way of doing things is the one to trust may not be the optimum method.

There are a number of methods of development, including:
  - The Agile method

  - The Waterfall method

  - The DevOps method

  - The Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment (CI/CD) method

  - A variety of other methods, some overlapping, others a mixture of pieces of other methods.

See <https://www.uptech.team/blog/software-development-methodologies>

**Note:** It is important that everyone involved in an organisation’s DTX is fully on board with what is going on and understand their part in it. A pivotal aspect of DTX is that it is organisation-wide, replacing a culture of using different methods to implement different business processes and applications; the latter often called ‘point solutions’.

### Major Considerations
#### Critical Success Factors (CSFs)
First of all, the process should take account of FUMPAS, *functionality, usability, manageability, performance, availability* and *security*. If the process is for a business change which affects customers, internal, external or general public, the relative importance of the FUMPAS edicts will vary for each of them, as will the effort and costs involved.

In particular, websites used by the public have to cater for the factors which cause them to abandon using the site and cater for them upfront in the design of the process and the resulting IT website.

Remember, quality is aptly defined as ‘*getting it right first time’* and also ‘*fit for purpose’*. Retro-correcting a website may not bring back the customers who went to another, competitive site, after their first negative experience.

Retro-correction in IT in general is an expensive exercise.

Secondly, think ‘customer’ during the DTX project; internal for productivity and job satisfaction, for external third parties for ‘being easy to do business with and for the public, a great experience at the website.

Happy customers tend to buy more and buy more often.

### Digital Transformation Inhibitors
*Summary*: Digital transformation inhibitors include a lack of strategic vision and leadership support, resistance to change from employees, legacy systems, inadequate skills and resources, and poor data quality. Other obstacles are unclear objectives, insufficient budget, security concerns, and a lack of effective change management strategies.

  - Lack of a clear vision by all involved, leading to lack of focus.

  - Insufficient leadership support as top-level buy-in is critical for driving initiatives forward and allocating resources. *No sponsor = failure* is an IT equation as true as *V = IR* (Ohm’s Law)

  - Non-cooperative decision-making. Organisational silos can hinder collaboration and the implementation of cohesive DTX strategies.

  - Resistance to change by employees

  - Lack of digital skills necessary for DTX

  - Poor user experience of tools and general DX adoption

  - Inadequate training and support, especially in new technology:

  - Legacy systems can sometimes be difficult to integrate with modern applications

  - Technology choices and failing to recognise the business activity it is meant to support and enhance

  - Security concerns, since more digital activity invites more normal services interruptions, especially cybersecurity breaches.

  - Poor data quality: Inaccurate, inconsistent, or siloed data is an obvious integration problem

  - Insufficient budget: A lack of financial resources can limit

  - Poor or absence of change management, a pivotal activity in any major IT activity

  - Inadequate IT governance or adherence to existing governance edits

  - Not everyone being on the same page of the DTX book

### DTX Summary
  - Digital transformation is the process of integrating digital technologies into all areas of a business, to improve operations, deliver value to customers, increase productivity, profitability and stay competitive in a fast-changing environment.

  - It is the opposite of ‘point’ solutions, where solutions are created by different methods on different systems or by different methods

  - It involves a change of thinking about the business and functioning by everyone in the organisation

  - Organisations that embrace digital transformation are better equipped to adapt to market changes, meet customer expectations, and drive growth. Success is necessary, as failure is the inverse of DTX and can leave the organisation worse off than before

  - It’s considered essential for long-term survival and success in today’s digital economy, which mandates it is done properly.

  - Doing it incorrectly is worse than not doing it at all and the former method has been proven to be a costly option and not only financially.
