## The problem

There is a long-running shortage of IT people with the skills the modern workplace needs. Research from the early 2020s put the proportion of IT vacancies that were difficult or impossible to fill at close to 80%, and the shortage has not gone away since.

That is odd, on the face of it. There are hundreds of IT courses and thousands of graduates. So the shortage is not a shortage of training. It is a mismatch between what is taught and what the workplace runs on.

## Why the mismatch happens

Most training paths start from computer science or from the PC and Windows. Both are legitimate, and neither covers the machines that carry the bulk of the world's work. Nothing in the mainstream syllabus addresses mainframe, enterprise or high performance computing, and the mainframe still handles a large share of the world's transactions.

Other courses cover topics in patches, with little connecting them. Assembling a picture of the whole field from extracts of a dozen courses is neither practical nor affordable, and it still does not produce someone who can follow the work as it moves.

## Job mutation

IT jobs mutate. The half-life of a technology skill is commonly put at three to five years, which means a good part of what a four-year degree teaches has moved on by graduation. AI is accelerating this: removing headcount from some roles, creating others.

A broad grounding is the defence. Specialisms are worth having, but they need something underneath them. Starting a specialism from nothing is like joining a special forces unit straight from an accountancy job.

## Why general before special

IT behaves like a body. The parts are connected, and a change in one shows up in another: put in a faster processor and the storage and the network have to keep up. That is why medicine trains generalists before specialists, and it is the same argument here.

Specialising without a view of the environment risks two things: obsolescence when the job mutates, and an inability to talk to colleagues about anything outside your own patch.

## Apprenticeships

Apprenticeships are becoming a serious alternative to a three-year degree: shorter, cheaper, and closer to the work. The gap is that none of them offer a comprehensive general IT grounding alongside the specialism. That is the gap this course is built for.
